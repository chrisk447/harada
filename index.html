History

First 48 hours

Delivery starts by making each promise something you can watch yourself do. Do these in order.

  1. Write down every promise in her words, the way you said them to her.
  2. For each, write what a camera would see, when it triggers, and a weekly minimum you'll hit even on bad weeks.
  3. Pick the three that matter most to her. The rest wait, each with a date to revisit.
  4. Put them on the Commitments tab.
  5. At the next trigger, do the behavior, log it right after, and say nothing.

When tension spikes

The order matters. Most of the damage happens in steps one and two.

  1. Breathe and wait three seconds. Don't defend, explain or counter.
  2. If you're flooded, say "I need 20 minutes, I'll be back at 8:15." Go to another room, calm down, and come back on time.
  3. When you return, reflect before you respond: "What I'm hearing is…" Keep going until she says that's right.
  4. Apologize for your part. Say what you did and how it landed. No "but."
  5. Within 24 hours, check in: "Are we okay?" Accept the answer she gives.
  6. Log it that night: what set you off, what you did, what you'll do next time.

If an argument starts around your daughter, say "Let's pick this up later" and take it elsewhere.

Sunday, alone

Five minutes by yourself. No one else is involved.

  • Open the Commitments tab: which minimums did you hit, which did you miss, and what got in the way? Adjust the trigger, not the promise.
  • Go through your promises: kept, missed, still open. If she would call it missed, it's missed.
  • Look back at the week's tension ratings and find the pattern: time of day, trigger, what you did.
  • Open the shared calendar and pick two or three tasks to offer her. Let her choose.
  • Write one change for the coming week on the Week tab.

Ask, offer, let her decide

Taking over without asking still decides for her, even when it's meant as help.

  • Ask first, with two options. "Chicken from the batch or the pasta?" is easy to answer. "What do you want for dinner?" gives her another decision.
  • Offer a choice. "Do you want me to take this, or do it together?"
  • Do it her way. Don't redo it or tweak it. Ask before changing anything.
  • Hear "I've got it" as information. Say "Okay, tell me if that changes."
  • Agree on lists once. "What do you want to hand me, and what do you want to keep?" A yes becomes standing until something changes.

Stage 2: Rebuild trust

Weeks 5 to 8, once tense moments are getting repaired within a day.

  • Keep your promise record clean for four weeks running: three open at most, each with a day.
  • Ask once a month: "Is there anything I said I'd do and haven't?" Then do it that week.
  • Offer the apology you wrote, once, when she seems open to hearing it.
  • Offer couples counseling once, with no pressure, and follow her lead. Book your own therapy now regardless.

Stage 3: Reconnect

Her call, not yours. Signs she's ready: she starts affection, plans something with you, or tells you. Then open the "Later: courtship" list on the Chart tab and start with a date night.

Rules against empty promises

  • Report, don't promise. Do the thing, then tell her you did it.
  • Never say yes on the spot. Say "Let me check my calendar and tell you by 3."
  • A promise is something you tell her you will do, with a day. The chart's daily habits are not promises.
  • Three open promises at most. Each has a day, written on your private list.
  • If you'll miss, say so before the deadline and give a new day. A silent miss is the one that costs trust.
  • Count honestly. You're the scorekeeper for now, so grade yourself the way she would.
  • Give it twelve weeks. Don't ask her to believe it. Let her watch.

Opening the conversation

Keep it short and ask for nothing. Something like:

"I know things have been hard, and I know I've made them harder. I'm not asking for anything right now. I'm getting help, and I'm working on being steadier. If there's one thing you need from me, I'd like to hear it whenever you're ready."
